Digital product, AI strategy, and enterprise platforms for global agricultural research

  • Laurens Goormachtigh
  • AgTech · Research · AI

Context

The Alliance of Bioversity International and CIAT is a CGIAR research organization working on sustainable food systems, crop diversity, and climate resilience. As Head of Product, I operate at the intersection of AgTech and research: building digital capabilities that connect data science, machine learning, and field-level innovation across multiple countries.

A flagship example of this direction is ONA, the first product from the ARTEMIS program—digital phenotyping that uses AI to analyze plant traits and support breeders’ decisions. The public-facing story of ONA and field tools such as Bruno is presented on ona.farm, which illustrates how smartphone-based capture, ML pipelines, and participatory approaches come together in the field.

The challenge

Before a consolidated product and delivery function, digital initiatives risked fragmented ownership, unclear prioritization, and uneven adoption across research programs and country teams. Scaling AI-enabled platforms, data infrastructure, and field applications required not only technology, but governance, lifecycle management, and alignment between technical teams and institutional stakeholders.

What I led

In the 2025–2026 period, I focused on establishing durable product practices and delivering integrated digital systems for the organization:

  • Digital product and delivery function. Established and operationalized the organization’s first structured digital product and delivery capability—introducing governance, prioritization, and lifecycle management for AI-enabled platforms.
  • Cross-functional delivery. Led cross-functional teams in the design and implementation of enterprise-grade digital systems, integrating machine learning pipelines, data infrastructure, and field-level applications across multiple countries.
  • Digital and AI strategy. Defined and executed strategy aligning platform development with organizational objectives, research priorities, and long-term scalability.
  • End-to-end architecture coordination. Coordinated how data pipelines, AI models, and user-facing applications fit together—supporting reliability, performance, and maintainability.
  • Stakeholder adoption. Introduced structured delivery and coordination across technical and non-technical stakeholders, strengthening institutional adoption and operational integration of digital systems.

Outcomes and reflection

The work moves the organization from ad hoc digital efforts toward repeatable product delivery and clear accountability for AI and platform investments—grounded in research impact and field usability. For me, this role underscores how product leadership in research organizations must combine strategy, systems thinking, and stakeholder change management—especially when AI and data products must work for scientists, partners, and farmers alike.
